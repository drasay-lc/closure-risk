Method, differences from the paper, and source code
  • Data. The data are IPEDS complete files for the 2002 to 2023 collections: directory, fall enrollment, staff, and FASB and for-profit finance. Units are 6-digit OPEIDs, covering private Title IV colleges. Dollars are in FY2023 terms, skewed levels are logged, and values are winsorized at 2.5%.
  • Features. These follow the paper:
    • enrollment, staff, revenue and expense levels, changes and shares
    • unrestricted net assets, plant debt, endowment
    • operating margin, days cash, EBIDA, debt/EBIDA, debt/assets
    • four distress flags
    They enter at 2- to 5-year lags.
  • Closures. These are dated from IPEDS directory records, not FSA's PEPS file, which could not be reached. There are about 1,840 private closures from 2004 to 2023, against the paper's 1,671 from 1996 to 2023, and both peak in 2016-18.
  • Not included. Federal heightened cash monitoring and financial responsibility scores, and county economic controls, are left out because their sources were unreachable. The paper finds county controls add little.
  • Validation. The train/test split is by institution, which is stricter than splitting by observation.
  • Scenarios. These start from 2023, not the paper's 2019. Revenue, expenses and staff scale with enrollment, and the fixed-cost variant is an addition to the paper.
  • Peer groups match the budget analysis site:
    • Group 1, L&C's comparison group
    • Group 2, its Carnegie class
    • Group 3, colleges with smaller endowments
  • Code and full outputs: github.com/drasay-lc/closure-risk (private repository). The paper: Philadelphia Fed WP 24-20.
